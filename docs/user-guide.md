# Work orders and asset release

Operations coordinators can create a draft work order directly or convert a submitted maintenance request into one. A draft is not an active repair. Critical priority requires a recorded reason and does not change asset availability. Search by work-order number, title or category, filter by status, then load more records when the target is older than the first page. Coordinators can edit administrative fields with a version-checked save; a conflict requires reloading and reviewing the current record.

Coordinators can assign technicians before publishing or scheduling work. The due date and planned work date are separate: a due date records when work is due, while scheduling records the planned date. The service requires an eligible active assignment before a start, a direct blocked-work resume, or a completed-work reopen enters in-progress. A blocked work order can be rescheduled without an assignment; assignment is required before its later start. Blocking requires a reason. Technicians can block in-progress work, but only an operations coordinator can resume blocked work to in progress or scheduled. An assigned technician can then start the scheduled job. Cancellation and reopening completed work require a reason and are recorded in work history; reopened work must be completed and submitted for review again.

An assigned technician can record checklist progress, notes, labor and private image evidence, declare the people who performed the repair, and submit the current revision for review. An operations coordinator who did not participate in the repair reviews that submitted revision. The coordinator can approve it or return it for correction. A returned or reopened repair can be updated and submitted as a new material revision; performers must be declared again for each revision. People recorded as performers or repair participants remain ineligible to review that work order even after assignment or role changes.

The dashboard places currently visible blocked, overdue and submitted work before its totals. Overdue uses the recorded due time, and counts and history reflect only records available to your current workspace access. Notifications belong to the signed-in recipient; opening a notification target rechecks current access to that exact record.

In Assets, coordinators can update recorded asset details, archive an asset while retaining its history, or reactivate an archived record. Archiving and reactivation do not change recorded availability or resolve safety holds. A printable QR label opens the authenticated asset route in this workspace; the label contains no access credentials. Repair approval records that the work passed review, but it does not release the asset. Resolve each active safety hold with its actual resolution, confirm the current condition and recorded checks, then record the separate release acknowledgment. The service rechecks release eligibility when the action is submitted.
